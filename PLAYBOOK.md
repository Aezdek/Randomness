# The iPod flipping playbook

Practical notes for buying used iPods, fixing them up and reselling them for a profit. Prices change all the time, so treat every number here as an example and check **eBay sold listings** (search, then filter by "Sold items") before you buy or price anything.

---

## 1. What to buy

The best margins come from iPods that need work most people can't do themselves.

| Priority | Model | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ⭐⭐⭐ | **iPod classic 6th/7th gen** (model A1238 on the back) | The most demand. Broken ones are cheap, and a flash + battery upgrade turns them into high-value units. The late-2009 160GB is the most wanted. |
| ⭐⭐⭐ | **iPod video 5th/5.5 gen** (A1136) | Big modding scene. Prefer **60GB or 80GB** units, which have 64MB of RAM and cope better with huge libraries than the 30GB (32MB). |
| ⭐⭐ | **iPod nano 7th gen** | Simple to sell, little work needed, steady demand. |
| ⭐⭐ | **iPod mini** | Nostalgic colours sell well. Flash mods are cheap. |
| ⭐ | **nano 3rd/4th/5th gen** | Fine as fillers and in bundles. Lower margin. |
| ⚠️ | **Shuffle** | Low prices, so shipping and fees eat the profit. Only buy in lots. |
| ⚠️ | **iPod touch** | Check **Activation Lock** before buying (5th gen and later). A locked touch is only worth its parts. |

## 2. Where to find deals

- **Search "for parts / not working".** A "sad iPod" face or a folder with an exclamation mark usually means a dead hard drive, which is exactly what a flash upgrade replaces.
- **Buy lots and bundles.** "iPod lot" and "box of old iPods" listings are often priced by people who don't know the market.
- **Search misspellings:** `ipood`, `i pod`, `ipod clasic`, `ipod classsic`.
- **Use saved searches with notifications** so you see new listings first. Good Buy It Now deals go within minutes.
- **Make offers.** Offer about 20–30% under the asking price on "Best Offer" listings. Many sellers just want it gone.
- **Look offline.** Facebook Marketplace, thrift stores, garage sales and family members' drawers are often cheaper than eBay. Buy offline, sell online.
- **Use the "Sell yours" section on your site.** People with a dead iPod in a drawer would rather get $20 from you than nothing.

## 3. Red flags when buying

- Stock photos, or no photo of the actual unit turned on
- "Untested" from a seller with lots of electronics (usually means "it's broken")
- iPod touch with no photo of the home screen (could be Activation Locked)
- Fakes: off fonts, wrong button layout, a "classic" with a touchscreen, prices far too low. Check that the model number on the back matches the model.
- Swollen battery (gaps at the seams, a bulging back). Still buyable as parts, but handle carefully and price that in.

## 4. Fixing and upgrading

The usual iPod classic/video rebuild:
1. **Storage:** replace the hard drive with an **iFlash** adapter (Solo = 1 SD card, Quad = up to 4) or a similar flash adapter, plus SD cards. Check compatibility for your exact model before ordering.
2. **Battery:** removing the hard drive frees space for a much bigger battery. Pair it with a **thick back plate** if it needs one.
3. **Cosmetics:** new faceplates, back plates and click wheels are cheap and can move a unit from Grade C to A, which is often worth a lot more than they cost.
4. **Tools:** proper iPod opening tools (the classic's clips are tight), a spudger and patience. The **headphone jack / hold switch ribbon cable** and the **battery connector** are the parts beginners break most. Pop the case open gently and slide it apart slowly.

Start with a cheap parts unit to practise on. Your first opening will probably bend something.

## 5. Test before you sell

Use the checklist on the website. It's also your real checklist. Extras:
- **Diagnostic mode (click-wheel iPods):** reset by holding **Menu + Center** until the Apple logo appears, then immediately hold **Center + Previous (⏮)**. On stock hard drives, check the SMART data (reallocated/pending sectors) and run the drive scan.
- **Disk mode:** reset, then hold **Center + Play**. Useful when an iPod won't restore normally.
- **Battery run-down:** fully charge it, loop an album and time it. Write the result in the listing.
- **Restore** with Finder (Mac) or iTunes (Windows) as the last step. Sell it **empty**.
- **Photograph the serial number** on every unit before you ship it (see section 9).

## 6. Listings that sell

**Title:** front-load the search words buyers use.
`iPod Classic 7th Gen 512GB Flash SD Mod New 3000mAh Battery Silver - Tested`

**Photos** (you can take these on your phone; aim for 8–12):
- Plain background with soft daylight and no flash
- Front with the screen **on**
- Settings › About screen showing capacity
- The back, at an angle that shows the real scratches
- Every flaw, close up. Honest photos are what prevent returns.

**Description:** model and generation, what was replaced, battery test result, grade, every flaw, what's in the box, and "wiped and restored". Short bullet points beat paragraphs.

**Pricing:**
- Price from **sold** listings, not active ones. Active listings are just what people wish they'd get.
- Use Buy It Now with Best Offer turned on, and set an auto-decline floor.
- Include shipping in the price ("free shipping") if you can. Buyers filter by it.

## 7. Fees and profit math

eBay takes roughly **13–15% of the total sale (including shipping) plus a small per-order fee** in most categories. Check eBay's fee page for your country and category.

Example with **illustrative numbers only**:

| | |
| --- | ---: |
| Dead iPod classic 7th gen (sad iPod, "for parts") | $45 |
| Flash adapter + SD card | $50 |
| Bigger battery + thick back | $25 |
| New faceplate | $10 |
| **Cost** | **$130** |
| Sells for | $260 |
| eBay fees (~14%) | –$36 |
| Shipping + box | –$12 |
| **Profit** | **≈ $82** for ~2 hours of work |

Track **every** purchase, part, fee and shipping label in a spreadsheet from day one. It's the only way to know which models are actually worth your time, and you'll need it for taxes.

## 8. Shipping

- Use a **small box with bubble wrap**, not a padded envelope. Click wheels and screens crack in mailers.
- Always buy shipping **with tracking**. Add signature confirmation on high-value units.
- Ship within 1–2 business days. Fast shipping improves your seller rating and search placement.

## 9. Protecting yourself

- **Serial-number swap scam:** a buyer "returns" a different, broken iPod. Photograph the serial and the unit's marks before shipping so you can prove it isn't yours.
- **"Not as described" returns** are the main risk on eBay. The cure is brutally honest descriptions and photos.
- **Offering 30-day returns** improves search placement, and very few people use them if the listing is accurate.
- **Direct sales from your website:** take payment by invoice (for example PayPal Goods & Services or a Stripe payment link), and only ship to the address on the payment. Never accept gift cards, never ship before payment clears, and never pay "release fees" to strangers.
- **Local sales:** cash or instant payment in a public place, and let the buyer test it there.

## 10. Rules that'll get you in trouble

- **Don't sell iPods with music loaded on them.** That's distributing copyrighted music, and eBay can remove the listing. Always sell them wiped.
- **Don't move eBay buyers off eBay.** No website links, phone numbers or "message me for a better price" in eBay listings or eBay messages. eBay suspends accounts for it. Promote your website on social media instead.
- **eBay and PayPal accounts require you to be 18+.** If you're younger, run it through a parent's or guardian's account with their involvement.
- **Taxes:** profit is income. Keep your records (section 7) and look up the rules where you live.

## 11. Growing it

- **Post the process.** Short before/after repair videos of iPods do very well on TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, and they send buyers to your site.
- **Reddit:** r/ipod is the main community. Read the rules first, and contribute before you post anything for sale.
- **Build a reputation on eBay first.** Feedback count is what makes buyers trust you on your own site.
- **Go deep before you go wide.** Get really good at one model (the classic) before you expand.
- **Reinvest early profits in parts stock** (batteries, faceplates, SD cards) so you can turn units around faster.
